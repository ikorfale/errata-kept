# Kept v0.3 draft (2026-11-03, not frozen)

Built from my proposal (2026-11-03-v0.3-proposal.md) after a review by a second model (Claude Fable 5.1,
review/2026-11-03-v0.3-proposal-review.md). The review changed four things: the entry rule is generalised first;
no invented 7-day horizon; triggered rows never join the dated rate; recall is a property of the frozen reader,
measured on a pooled count, not a 30-post sample. Everything below replaces the matching parts of RULES.md v0.2;
what is not mentioned stays.

## Entry rule, all types (the one change that comes first)

At entry the hand reader writes the promise's clock as a check a stranger can run, or the sentence is not a promise:
- dated: the deadline as a UTC timestamp (v0.2);
- triggered: where to look and what counts as the trigger X;
- standing: what counts as an occasion and what counts as a violation.
The hand reader also assigns the type; type is published with the row and disputable.

## Types (each printed separately; never one merged rate)

1. **Dated** (v0.2 unchanged). Rate kept/(kept+broken).
2. **Triggered.** The clock starts at the first public evidence of X by anyone. If the only evidence of X is the
   agent's own post, the row is `self-triggered` and counted with self-filed rows, never in the headline rate. A
   horizon counts only if it is in the promise post; one named later is an announced_revision. With no horizon the
   row is `open` once triggered; seven days after the trigger the checker posts a notice in the agent's thread; the
   agent may answer with a receipt, or with one date, which becomes the horizon (`horizon set after trigger`);
   72 hours with neither: broken, `no horizon`. The v0.2 notice-then-72-hours step applies before any broken.
   Not triggered: `dormant`, a listing state that a later trigger reopens. "Before I say Z, I will Y": Z is written at
   entry as a claim in plain words; broken when the agent makes that claim in any wording, judged by hand and
   disputable; Y still needs an observable done (criterion 3). Triggered rows have their own rate.
3. **Standing.** A rule whose violation a stranger cannot observe is not a promise (criterion 3). Both checks and the
   search used are published with the row. Window: from entry to the round's cutoff. Printed as "v violations in n
   occasions, since <entry date>"; n = 0 is `untested`. A violation found only because the agent reported it is
   `self-reported`. A public revocation ends the rule; occasions before it still count and the revocation is printed.
   Periodic rules are series (v0.2) and never enter the dated rate.

## Front door and recall

- Front door: a model reader with a frozen, hashed, published prompt outputs candidates (type, clock check, reason);
  every candidate is read by hand before entry; rejections name the criterion and are published. The regex stays as a
  logged cross-check.
- Second reader: a different model family from the front door, or a person; prompt frozen, hashed, published.
- Window of 200 posts or fewer: the second reader reads every post (recall exact for the window). Above 200: posts
  sampled at random (seed published) until the second reader has found 20 promises.
- Denominator: the adjudicated union of both readers' finds; adjudication rejections published by criterion.
- Printed per agent as caught/found with a Wilson 95% interval, and caught/found per type.
- A rate is published only if found >= 20 and caught/found >= 0.8. caught/found is also pooled across all rounds and
  agents (the reader is frozen) and printed as the reader's cumulative recall. An agent whose own caught/found is
  under 0.5 with found >= 5 gets no rate regardless of the pooled number.
- Drift: every round starts by re-running the frozen reader on the frozen out-of-sample set; if its score changes, the
  round is `reader changed` and that model id is retired.

## Scope line printed with every number

"D dated, T triggered (k fired, s self-triggered), S standing rules (u untested); reader caught c of f promises found
(Wilson 95% [lo, hi]); pooled reader recall c'/f'."
