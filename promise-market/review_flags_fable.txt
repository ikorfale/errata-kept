**Short answer: no bug that changes the numbers, but two confounds and one design choice that change what the numbers mean.**

1. **Code is correct for what it claims.** Extraction, audit, ring buffer, fitness, imitation, and the fsweep/audonly/wcheck drivers (which set e, W via a copy of P) all do what the docstring says. The CLI `one`/`sweep` cannot set e, W or reply, but your e=0.3 'aud' and W=60 runs came from audonly.py and wcheck.py, so they are reproducible.

2. **Reply corrects extractor flags too, and that is the whole with-reply result.** Line 61 uses `checked` (extract | audited), so every kept promise, however checked, is recorded kept. K's ledger becomes noise-free: rep is exactly (n+1)/(n+2). Noise survives only as broken-to-kept flips, which help R and X. So "reply" is not "contest audit flags"; it is "noise applies only to broken promises". The mechanism is no longer signal-to-noise: an X with the same record count loses to K as soon as one broken audit sits in its window. Per record that happens with probability q0(1-kx)(1-e)/checkrate, so at r=0.3 the chance a 20-record X has zero broken records is about 0.22 at q0=0.05 and 0.06 at q0=0.1. That matches 0.46 vs 0.88. If you meant reply to apply to audits only, change `checked` to `audited` in the `ff` line; K stays noisy and the dilution story applies with per-audit gap 0.7(1-kx) instead of 0.4(1-kx).

3. **rc accounting is fine.** Charged to the promiser on checked, kept, flipped records only. K pays about 0.02 per promise, X slightly less, R nothing. Negligible against c=1.

4. **Confound in r=0.3 vs r=0.5: kx=r.** Raising r also raises the mimic's keep share, so each audit of X is less informative: per-audit gap 0.4(1-kx) drops 0.28 to 0.20. Audit share among checks drops 0.45 to 0.33. Each factor is about 0.72, so roughly half of the r effect is the mimic keeping more, not dilution. Test: fix kx=0.3 at r=0.5 and kx=0.5 at r=0.3.

5. **Confound in aud vs rep: the window cap.** Popular agents are picked many times per generation, so the pooled ledger hits W=20 and holds only about W times share audit records (4 to 7), while ledA for the same agent keeps all its audits and never fills. Part of "aud beats rep" is eviction, not dilution. The W=60 jump from 0.49 to 0.65 shows the cap binds. To isolate dilution, run both choosers with W large enough never to fill within a generation.

6. **Interpretation: directionally right, incomplete.** Extractor records of a mimic are Bernoulli(1-e) for both K and X, so they carry zero K-vs-X information, and pooling them adds variance with no gap. The discriminability is about sqrt(audit count) times sqrt(audit share) times (1-kx). So share matters through a square root, absolute audit count matters as much, and kx matters independently. "Higher recall raises the audit rate needed" holds here partly because kx tracks r.

7. **Minor, not a bug.** With a0=1, rep grows with record count, so a more-checked clean agent outranks a less-checked clean one, and ledgers reset every generation. Under reply this is the only way an X with one broken audit can beat a K (the K must have fewer than about half as many records). With the 'aud' chooser at q0=0.1 agents have about 3 audit records, so the prior dominates scores there.
