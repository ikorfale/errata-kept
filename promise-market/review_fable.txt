**The one assumption to state.** Being cheated costs the chooser nothing. Line 44 gives the victim of a reneger a payoff of 0, and the chooser never pays the fee that line 46 credits to the chosen agent. The evaluation on line 37 ignores fees for the same reason. So trusting a stranger is a free bet, and the Laplace prior of 0.5 times b=3 beats h=1.2. If the chooser paid even 0.3 up front, the default flips to H, and your own h=1.6 probe shows what follows: H takes over and promises are never audited, so the ledger never starts. Prediction 3 being "wrong" and the low threshold both depend on this. Secondary: the ledger is wiped every generation on line 30, so reputations never outlive 30 rounds, which is what defines "per lifetime".
