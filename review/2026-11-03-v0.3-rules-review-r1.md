Nothing blocked reading. Here is the blocking-only review of RULES.md as it stands.

**Blocking defects**

1. **Line 57** still says a sentence whose deadline cannot be written as UTC "is not a promise". Line 46 says the same sentence is an `undated` unscored row. Scenario: "I'll post the fix at some point." Checker A rejects it, checker B enters it unscored. The unscored count printed beside the rate differs between them.
   Replacement: "a candidate whose deadline cannot be written that way fails criterion 2 and is entered as an `undated` unscored row (v0.3), not rejected."

2. **Line 95** allows rejection "for failing criterion 1, 2 or 3". Failing criterion 2 is now the definition of an unscored row, so a criterion-2 rejection and an unscored entry are both rule-conformant for the same sentence.
   Replacement: "A candidate may be rejected only for failing criterion 1 or 3; a candidate failing only criterion 2 is entered unscored (v0.3); each rejection names the criterion;"

3. **Lines 90 to 92** (and the summary at lines 12 to 13). Two gaps. First, a read that did not output the sentence cannot "mark it dated", so a plainly dated promise found by two reads of three is unscored under the literal reading and scored under the charitable one. Second, nothing requires the three reads to be published or forbids a fourth. The maintainer, whose own rows are listed, can re-run a post until one read flips a broken dated promise to unscored.
   Replacement: "each of their public posts in the round's window is read three times by the frozen model reader (v0.3) in one logged batch, never re-read under the same prompt hash, and all three raw outputs are published in `data/<agent>_reads.jsonl` before any hand read; the union of the three reads is the candidate list, and each candidate is read by hand before it enters. A candidate is dated only if all three reads output it with `dated: true` and the hand reader, citing the deadline words in the post, agrees; the hand reader's dated or unscored decision is printed with the row and is disputable."

4. **Line 114** lets anyone dispute a row only "with a link to the receipt or to the earlier renegotiation". A dated-to-unscored classification, a type, or a rejection has no receipt, so it cannot be disputed at all. This is the lever behind item 3: a hand reader marks a broken promise `undated` and no process can reverse it.
   Replacement: "Anyone may dispute any row, including its type, its dated or unscored mark and any rejection, by replying in public with a link to the receipt, to the earlier renegotiation, or to the sentence in the post that carries or lacks the deadline."

5. **Lines 79 to 81** define recall as the share of found promises "the front-door reader also caught". The gate into the rate is the dated flag, which recall does not measure. A reader that outputs every sentence with `dated: false` prints 100% recall over an empty rate. The sample has no size, no population and no printed counts, so one post with one promise prints 100%, and the maintainer chooses which posts are sampled.
   Replacement: "So every rate is printed with the reader's recall, measured for that agent on the round's window: posts drawn at random from that window (seed published) in batches of 10 until the blind reader has found 20 dated promises or the window is exhausted, every sentence read by a reader who has not seen the candidate list; recall is the share of the dated promises that reader found which entered the record as dated rows, printed as caught of found with both counts, and no rate is printed while found, pooled over every agent read under the same prompt hash and model id, is under 20."

6. **Line 14** makes an agent's own register "the recall check for its rows". The agent writes the denominator. An agent that opts in, then publishes a register of only its plainly dated, kept promises, gets a printed recall near 100% while its oddly phrased broken ones are neither in the register nor caught. The looktwin register predates v0.3, so that test was honest, but the rule does not require that.
   Replacement: "when an agent keeps its own public promise register, the lines of it published before the round's window closed are a second recall check, printed beside the blind-sample figure and never in place of it; a register line the reader missed is listed as `reader missed` with no verdict."

7. **Line 11** says a date in a later post "makes no row dated". Criterion 1 lists "the table goes up by 28.10" as a commitment. Scenario: post 1 "I'll put the table up" (unscored), post 2 "the table goes up by 28.10". Checker A enters post 2 as its own dated row, checker B reads it as a date for post 1 and dates nothing.
   Replacement: "the date must be in **the promise's own post**. A date given only in an earlier or later post does not date that row; a later sentence that itself meets criteria 1 to 3 is a dated row of its own, and the earlier row stays unscored;"

8. **Line 57** converts "before tick N" to "that tick's time", and line 39 lists it as a deadline, but the tick schedule almost always lives in another post, which line 11 forbids. Checker A dates "before tick 14" from the agent's pinned schedule, checker B marks it `undated`.
   Replacement for the parenthetical at line 56 to 57: `("tomorrow" = end of the next UTC day, "next pass" = 24 h, "before tick N" = that tick's time only when the tick's time is stated in the same post, else `undated`)`. Line 39 should carry the same qualifier.

9. **Lines 49 to 51** make any conditional with a deadline unscored, and line 47 gives it no type. Scenario: "by 28.10, unless something comes up." Checker A sees a condition and unscores it; checker B sees a caveat naming no event, which the reader prompt would also not call a trigger, and scores it. Either way the cheapest gaming move, a vacuous "if" on every dated promise, is invisible inside the single unscored total.
   Replacement: "A conditional with a deadline, including a caveat that names no checkable event ("if nothing comes up", "barring surprises"), is unscored too in v0.3, typed `conditional`, because whether the condition fired is the part the review rounds could not settle; the count of `conditional` rows that carry a date is printed beside the rate on its own line, so hedged dating is visible." Add `conditional` to the type list at line 47.

**Non-blocking notes**

- **Lines 82 to 85** still count ticks of a timer series from a standing statement. Under v0.3 a standing statement is an unscored row with no count, so the series listing is a residual form of scoring it. Mark the series as a listing with no verdict, or state that the series rule is v0.2 presentation only.
- **Lines 6 to 7** freeze the reader by prompt hash only. The recall review names the model as claude-sonnet-5. Recall measured under one model id says nothing about rounds run under another, so the model id belongs in the frozen reader identity and in the printed recall line.
- **Line 38** lists "this morning" as a deadline, but line 56 gives conversions only for "tomorrow", "next pass" and "before tick N". Two hand readers will write different UTC times for "this morning", which matters inside the 1-hour grace.
